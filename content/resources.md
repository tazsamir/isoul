These are links I find useful. Start small: choose one problem, one service and one backup plan.

## Self-hosting and privacy

- [Awesome Self-Hosted](https://github.com/awesome-selfhosted/awesome-selfhosted) — a large catalogue of free and open-source self-hosted software.
- [Privacy Guides: Self-hosting](https://www.privacyguides.org/en/self-hosting/) — practical privacy-focused guidance.
- [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/) — find alternatives to commercial software.
- [Docker documentation](https://docs.docker.com/) — containers and compose.

## People and forums worth following

These are sources to learn from, not a claim that I have tested every app or build they discuss. **Last checked: 16 September 2026.** The activity links lead to new posts; the notes below are a dated snapshot, not an automatically updated feed.

### Wendell — Level1Techs

[Level1Techs forum](https://forum.level1techs.com/) · [Wendell’s latest topics](https://forum.level1techs.com/u/wendell/activity/topics)

- [Self-Hosting Series in 2026: what are you running, and what are your pain points?](https://forum.level1techs.com/t/self-hosting-series-in-2026-what-are-you-running-whats-your-pain-points/249411) — Wendell’s April 2026 discussion about making self-hosting usable for a household: private access, HTTPS, shared logins, backups and the maintenance burden. A useful starting point before choosing more apps.
- [Buddy Backup, Part 2](https://forum.level1techs.com/t/249996) — May 2026 follow-up in his backup series.
- [Frigate NVR on Proxmox LXC with NVIDIA](https://forum.level1techs.com/t/253682) — August 2026 troubleshooting notes for a specific camera-recording setup, covering intermittent errors, video acceleration and detector crashes. Useful detail, but not a beginner default stack.

### Tom Lawrence — Lawrence Systems

[Lawrence Systems forum](https://forums.lawrencesystems.com/) · [Tom’s latest topics](https://forums.lawrencesystems.com/u/LTS_Tom/activity/topics)

- [My Self Hosted Home Lab Software 2026](https://forums.lawrencesystems.com/t/my-self-hosted-home-lab-software-2026/27035) — Tom’s September 2026 inventory, with the opening post updated on 15 September. Covers virtualization and storage, remote access, monitoring, documents, photos and everyday tools, with links to projects and related videos.
- Useful areas to explore include XCP-ng and Proxmox, TrueNAS, NetBird, Paperless-ngx, Immich, FreshRSS and Docker management. This is **his setup**, not a shopping list or a list of services everyone needs to run.

### JDM_WAAAT — SERVERBUILDS.net

[SERVERBUILDS.net forum](https://forums.serverbuilds.net/) · [Latest community discussions](https://forums.serverbuilds.net/latest) · [JDM_WAAAT’s activity](https://forums.serverbuilds.net/u/JDM_WAAAT/activity)

- [NAS Killer 6.0 — DDR4 is finally cheap](https://forums.serverbuilds.net/t/guide-nas-killer-6-0-ddr4-is-finally-cheap/13956) — the newest numbered NAS Killer guide I could verify. Originally published in September 2023; the opening post shows an update on 10 March 2026, and JDM_WAAAT also replied that day. It focuses on inexpensive used hardware, DDR4 and Intel Quick Sync-capable processors. I did not find a newer NAS Killer 7.0 guide.
- [SNAFU, one generation later](https://forums.serverbuilds.net/t/15708) — a July 2026 **community build by TacoQuest**, not a new JDM_WAAAT guide. A detailed used-Xeon/DDR4 rack-server rebuild for readers who need expansion rather than a small, low-power box.

Old build guides are useful for the approach, but their prices and buying links age. Compare current UK availability, electricity use, noise, drive bays and replacement parts before buying. A cheap used enterprise server is not necessarily cheap to leave running. NAS Killer 6.0 is written around Unraid, which is paid software; check the requirements of your chosen NAS operating system separately. SERVERBUILDS.net also discloses affiliate links.
