> **Previously on isoul.uk:** [From a Charity-Shop DVD to My Television](/off-the-stack/from-a-charity-shop-dvd-to-my-television/) — making a disc easy to watch again was one way to get more from something I already owned. The older tablets deserved a similar look.

I have a Galaxy Tab A7 Lite. It's the tablet I pick up to browse the web or watch something. The other tablet in the house is a Galaxy Tab A8, used for news, social media, Facebook Marketplace and YouTube. Neither has an unusual job to do. We just want them to respond when we use them.

They had started to feel slow. That can make replacing them seem like the obvious answer, but I wanted to try making the ones we already own more pleasant first. There's a difference between a tablet that genuinely can't do what you need and one that's busy carrying a load of things you never asked it to do.

We went through the Samsung software with that in mind. Using a computer and Android's debugging tools, we disabled a selection of unwanted packages and set up a simpler launcher. We also changed the animation speed. It wasn't a case of wiping everything remotely connected to Samsung: the useful apps and the things we rely on needed to stay. The aim was a lighter everyday setup, not a tablet stripped so bare that it became inconvenient.

The result is a simpler setup on both tablets. I use the A7 Lite for browsing and watching things, so those everyday jobs are the test that matters to me. The A8 still needs to be useful for its own mix of news, social apps, Marketplace and YouTube. There'd be little point in trying to speed it up by removing the very things it's used for.

This was a change to the tablets we already had, rather than a rush to replace them. It isn't a promise that an older, modest tablet can suddenly do everything a new one can. The next test is simply whether these setups stay useful for the ordinary things we do on each tablet.
