Some people name servers after their function.

`docker01`.

`nas01`.

`backup02`.

That is sensible.

I name my machines after spaceships from stories I love.

This is less sensible.

I recommend it.

## A homelab is already unnecessary enough

Most of what I run at home could be replaced with hosted services.

That means the homelab isn't only infrastructure. It is also a hobby.

Once I accepted that, there wasn't much reason to pretend the machines needed enterprise naming conventions.

I wanted names I would actually enjoy seeing.

Science fiction supplied plenty.

## The pattern is spaceships

I kept seeing TrueNAS examples with storage pools called `tank`, and sometimes `dozer`. In a path such as `/mnt/tank`, that's the pool name, not the name of a dataset inside it. According to a [TrueNAS community account](https://www.truenas.com/community/threads/how-to-first-configuration-for-small-freenas-deployments.27062/), those names came from Tank and Dozer in *The Matrix*: a bit of personality tucked into storage documentation. That stuck with me: if a pool can have a name from a film, why should my computers be `server01`?

My own pattern is spaceships. The ships come from different stories I like, including *Star Trek* and *Mass Effect*, but the idea is consistent. I pick a name that feels right for the machine rather than a number that tells me nothing about it.

I care that the names are memorable and enjoyable. A good naming scheme should survive contact with things you like.

## Names help more than I expected

There is actually one practical benefit.

I remember names more easily than numbered hosts.

Mentioning one of those names is instantly clearer in my head than referring to another numbered host.

That matters once a homelab grows beyond a single PC under a desk.

## The names connect the hobby to everything before it

The part I like most is that the naming scheme accidentally connects different periods of my life.

I grew up watching science fiction because my older siblings watched it.

Later I got deeply into anime.

Then I became the sort of adult who runs Linux servers at home for fun.

Now those servers are named after things I watched decades earlier.

None of that was planned.

But it makes the whole setup feel more personal.

And if I am going to spend an evening troubleshooting a machine that refuses to behave, I would much rather be angry at a fictional spacecraft than `server01`.
